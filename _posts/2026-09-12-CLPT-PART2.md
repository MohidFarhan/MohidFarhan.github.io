## Introduction

In our last exploration, we derived the continuity equation—the statement that mass is conserved as it flows through expanding spacetime. We watched how the density contrast $$\delta$$ evolves due to the convergence of fluid flow and the stretching of conformal spacetime itself. But continuity alone cannot build structure; it tells us only that matter redistributes, not *why* it moves.

Today, we derive the **Euler equation** for Cold Dark Matter. If the continuity equation is the accounting of mass, the Euler equation is the physics of force. It tells us how gravitational potentials accelerate fluid elements, how the cosmic expansion dampens peculiar velocities, and how momentum conservation shapes the cosmic web.

We proceed from where we stood before. In Blog 17 we adopted **conformal time** $$\eta$$ (with $$d\eta = dt/a$$), the Newtonian gauge with potentials $$\phi = \psi$$, and defined the velocity divergence $$\theta \equiv \partial_i v^i$$ using the **comoving** peculiar velocity $$v^i = dx^i/d\eta$$. We derived the continuity equation in that convention:

$$
\delta' + \theta - 3\phi' = 0
$$

with primes denoting $$d/d\eta$$. Today we turn to the spatial component of the conservation law, $$\nabla_\mu T^{\mu i} = 0$$, and derive its dynamical partner.

## Conventions Recap

Since notation is everything in perturbation theory, let us restate the essential conventions inherited from Blog 17:

- **Metric signature**: $$(-,+,+,+)$$.
- **Time coordinate**: conformal time $$\eta$$, with $$d\eta = dt/a$$. Primes denote $$\partial_\eta$$.
- **Newtonian gauge metric**:

$$
ds^2 = a^2(\eta)\left[-(1+2\phi)d\eta^2 + (1-2\phi)\delta_{ij}dx^i dx^j\right] \tag{48}
$$

- **Conformal Hubble parameter**: $$\mathcal{H} \equiv a'/a = aH$$.
- **Background density**: satisfies $$\bar{\rho}' + 3\mathcal{H}\bar{\rho} = 0$$, so $$\bar{\rho} \propto a^{-3}$$.
- **Comoving peculiar velocity**: $$v^i \equiv dx^i/d\eta$$ (a first-order perturbation).
- **Velocity divergence**: $$\theta \equiv \partial_i v^i$$.

## The Setup: Spatial Components of Conservation

The conservation of energy-momentum in General Relativity is expressed through the covariant divergence:

$$
\nabla_\mu T^{\mu i} = 0 \tag{49}
$$

In Blog 17 we examined the temporal component ($$\nu = 0$$) and obtained the continuity equation. Now we set $$\nu = i$$, where $$i$$ runs over the three spatial indices $$(1, 2, 3)$$. This gives us the evolution of momentum density—the Euler equation.

Expanding the covariant derivative:

$$
\nabla_\mu T^{\mu i} = \partial_\mu T^{\mu i} + \Gamma^\mu_{\mu\lambda}T^{\lambda i} + \Gamma^i_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda} = 0 \tag{50}
$$

For CDM (a pressureless perfect fluid),

$$
T^{\mu\nu} = \rho\, u^\mu u^\nu \tag{51}
$$

where $$\rho = \bar{\rho}(1+\delta)$$ is the total density and $$u^\mu$$ is the 4-velocity.

## The 4-Velocity in Conformal Newtonian Gauge

From the normalization condition $$u_\mu u^\mu = -1$$ applied to the metric (48), the 4-velocity of a CDM element with comoving peculiar velocity $$v^i$$ is, to first order,

$$
u^\mu = \frac{1}{a}\left(1 - \phi,\; v^i\right), \qquad
u_\mu = a\left(-(1+\phi),\; v_i\right) \tag{52}
$$

The factor of $$1/a$$ in $$u^\mu$$ reflects the fact that $$\eta$$ is a conformal time coordinate, and the perturbation $$-\phi$$ in the time component is the gravitational time dilation. It is straightforward to verify $$u^\mu u_\mu = -1 + \mathcal{O}(2)$$.

## The Christoffel Symbols in Conformal Newtonian Gauge

From the metric (48), the non-zero Christoffel symbols to first order in $$\phi$$ are:

$$
\Gamma^0_{00} = \mathcal{H} + \phi', \qquad
\Gamma^0_{0i} = \partial_i\phi, \qquad
\Gamma^0_{ij} = \left[\mathcal{H}(1-2\phi) - \phi'\right]\delta_{ij} \tag{53}
$$

$$
\Gamma^i_{00} = \partial^i\phi, \qquad
\Gamma^i_{0j} = \left(\mathcal{H} - \phi'\right)\delta^i_j, \qquad
\Gamma^i_{jk} = -\delta^i_k\partial_j\phi - \delta^i_j\partial_k\phi + \delta_{jk}\partial^i\phi \tag{54}
$$

Of these, only $$\Gamma^0_{00}$$, $$\Gamma^0_{0i}$$, $$\Gamma^0_{ij}$$, $$\Gamma^i_{00}$$, and $$\Gamma^i_{0j}$$ will contribute to the Euler equation at linear order. Note the appearance of $$\mathcal{H}$$ inside $$\Gamma^0_{00}$$ and $$\Gamma^i_{0j}$$—these are the conformal-time analogue of the $$H$$ terms we saw in Blog 17, and they will combine into a **Hubble drag** below.

## Evaluating the Terms

We now compute each piece of (50), keeping only linear terms in the perturbations.

### The Partial Derivative Term $$\partial_\mu T^{\mu i}$$

This splits into time and space parts:

$$
\partial_\mu T^{\mu i} = \partial_0 T^{0i} + \partial_j T^{ji} \tag{55}
$$

First, $$T^{0i} = \rho u^0 u^i$$. Using (52) and keeping only first-order terms:

$$
T^{0i} = \rho \cdot \frac{1-\phi}{a}\cdot\frac{v^i}{a} \approx \frac{\bar{\rho}\, v^i}{a^2} \tag{56}
$$

where we drop the product $$\phi v^i$$ (second order) and replace $$\rho \to \bar{\rho}$$ in a first-order term.

Now take the conformal-time derivative. Using $$\bar{\rho}' = -3\mathcal{H}\bar{\rho}$$:

$$
\partial_0 T^{0i} = \partial_\eta\!\left(\frac{\bar{\rho}\,v^i}{a^2}\right)
= \frac{\bar{\rho}' v^i}{a^2} + \frac{\bar{\rho}\,v'^i}{a^2} - \frac{2a'\bar{\rho}\,v^i}{a^3}
= \frac{\bar{\rho}}{a^2}\left(v'^i - 5\mathcal{H}v^i\right) \tag{57}
$$

Next, the spatial derivative term:

$$
T^{ji} = \rho\, u^j u^i = \rho\,\frac{v^j}{a}\frac{v^i}{a} \tag{58}
$$

This is second order in $$v$$, so we set:

$$
\partial_j T^{ji} \approx 0 \tag{59}
$$

### The Christoffel Contraction $$\Gamma^\mu_{\mu\lambda}T^{\lambda i}$$

This contracts the trace of the Christoffel symbols with the momentum density:

$$
\Gamma^\mu_{\mu\lambda}T^{\lambda i} = \Gamma^\mu_{\mu 0}T^{0i} + \Gamma^\mu_{\mu j}T^{ji} \tag{60}
$$

The second term involves $$T^{ji}$$, which is second order. For the first, we need the trace:

$$
\Gamma^\mu_{\mu 0} = \Gamma^0_{00} + \Gamma^j_{j0} = \left(\mathcal{H}+\phi'\right) + 3\left(\mathcal{H}-\phi'\right) = 4\mathcal{H} - 2\phi' \tag{61}
$$

where we used (53) and (54) with $$j$$ summed. Multiplying by $$T^{0i}$$ (first order), the $$-2\phi'$$ piece is second order and drops:

$$
\Gamma^\mu_{\mu 0}T^{0i} \approx 4\mathcal{H}\cdot\frac{\bar{\rho}\,v^i}{a^2} \tag{62}
$$

### The Christoffel Curvature Term $$\Gamma^i_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda}$$

This is the term that will supply the gravitational force:

$$
\Gamma^i_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda}
= \Gamma^i_{00}T^{00} + \Gamma^i_{0j}T^{0j} + \Gamma^i_{j0}T^{j0} + \Gamma^i_{jk}T^{jk} \tag{63}
$$

The last term has $$\Gamma^i_{jk}$$ (first order) contracted with $$T^{jk} \sim v^j v^k$$ (second order), so it drops.

**First piece:** Using $$\Gamma^i_{00} = \partial^i\phi$$ and $$T^{00} = \rho (u^0)^2 \approx \bar{\rho}/a^2$$ (since $$\Gamma^i_{00}$$ is already first order, we take the zeroth-order piece of $$T^{00}$$):

$$
\Gamma^i_{00}T^{00} \approx \frac{\bar{\rho}}{a^2}\,\partial^i\phi \tag{64}
$$

**Second and third pieces:** Using $$\Gamma^i_{0j} = (\mathcal{H}-\phi')\delta^i_j$$:

$$
\Gamma^i_{0j}T^{0j} = (\mathcal{H}-\phi')\frac{\bar{\rho}\,v^i}{a^2} \approx \frac{\mathcal{H}\bar{\rho}\,v^i}{a^2} \tag{65}
$$

and by symmetry $$\Gamma^i_{j0} = \Gamma^i_{0j}$$, so

$$
\Gamma^i_{j0}T^{j0} \approx \frac{\mathcal{H}\bar{\rho}\,v^i}{a^2} \tag{66}
$$

Collecting:

$$
\Gamma^i_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda} \approx \frac{\bar{\rho}}{a^2}\left(\partial^i\phi + 2\mathcal{H}v^i\right) \tag{67}
$$

## Assembling the Euler Equation

Substituting (57), (62), and (67) into (50) and multiplying through by $$a^2/\bar{\rho}$$:

$$
\left(v'^i - 5\mathcal{H}v^i\right) + 4\mathcal{H}v^i + \partial^i\phi + 2\mathcal{H}v^i = 0 \tag{68}
$$

The Hubble terms combine as $$-5\mathcal{H} + 4\mathcal{H} + 2\mathcal{H} = +\mathcal{H}$$, so:

$$
v'^i + \mathcal{H}v^i + \partial^i\phi = 0 \tag{69}
$$

Rearranging to the standard form:

$$
\boxed{v'^i + \mathcal{H}v^i = -\partial^i\phi} \tag{70}
$$

This is the **Euler equation** for CDM in conformal time. It states that the peculiar acceleration ($$v'^i$$) plus the Hubble drag ($$\mathcal{H}v^i$$) equals the negative gradient of the gravitational potential. The physics is transparent: gravity pushes matter down potential gradients, while cosmic expansion stretches velocities apart.

## The Velocity Divergence Form

In cosmological perturbation theory, we usually work with the **velocity divergence** $$\theta \equiv \partial_i v^i$$ rather than the vector field itself. Taking the divergence of (70):

$$
\partial_i v'^i + \mathcal{H}\partial_i v^i = -\partial_i\partial^i\phi \tag{71}
$$

Assuming derivatives commute:

$$
\theta' + \mathcal{H}\theta = -\nabla^2\phi \tag{72}
$$

In Fourier space, where $$\nabla^2 \to -k^2$$:

$$
\theta' + \mathcal{H}\theta = k^2\phi \tag{73}
$$

Note the sign: in gravitational wells ($$\phi < 0$$), the right-hand side is negative, indicating that overdensities induce infall ($$\theta < 0$$), consistent with gravitational collapse.

## Coupling with the Continuity Equation

Together with the conformal-time continuity equation from Blog 17:

$$
\delta' + \theta - 3\phi' = 0 \tag{74}
$$

the Euler equation forms a closed system describing the evolution of density and velocity perturbations in CDM. Note that the two equations share the same $$\theta$$, defined with the comoving velocity $$v^i = dx^i/d\eta$$. This is the principal advantage of the conformal-time convention: the continuity and Euler equations form a symmetric pair, with no explicit factors of $$a$$ appearing in the couplings.

These two equations, combined with the Einstein equations (which relate $$\phi$$ to $$\delta$$ through the Poisson equation), completely determine the linear growth of structure. In the next installment, we will solve this coupled system to find the **growth factor** $$D(a)$$, which tells us how primordial fluctuations seeded in the early universe grow into the large-scale structure we observe today.

## Summary

Beginning from the spatial components of the conservation equation $$\nabla_\mu T^{\mu i} = 0$$ in conformal Newtonian gauge, and carefully expanding each term using the 4-velocity (52) and the Christoffel symbols (53)–(54), we have derived:

$$
\boxed{v'^i + \mathcal{H}v^i = -\partial^i\phi} \tag{75}
$$

or equivalently, for the velocity divergence:

$$
\boxed{\theta' + \mathcal{H}\theta = k^2\phi} \tag{76}
$$

These equations capture the competition between gravitational infall (the potential gradient) and cosmic expansion (the Hubble drag). They are the dynamical engine that drives structure formation, transforming the static gravitational potential wells into flowing rivers of dark matter that eventually coalesce into galaxies.
