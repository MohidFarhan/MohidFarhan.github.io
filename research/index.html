Research Summary

Overview of my research in dark-sector physics and cosmology.

Overview

My research sits at the intersection of particle physics and cosmology, focusing on the nature of the dark sector, the ~95% of the universe’s energy budget that remains unexplained by the Standard Model. I work on both the microscopic (field-theoretic) and macroscopic (cosmological-observable) sides of the problem, using the former to construct well-motivated models and the latter to test them against data.

Research Themes

1. Interacting Dark Energy

I study models where dark matter and dark energy exchange energy or momentum through symmetry-protected portals. In particular, I have shown that pure momentum exchange, with no background energy transfer, can arise naturally from a pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone-boson dark energy sector coupled to an inert scalar doublet, and that such models possess an intrinsic limit on their suppression of the clustering amplitude $ \sigma_8 $. This provides a theoretically controlled benchmark for interacting dark energy scenarios.

2. Layered Dark Sectors and the $ S_8 $ Tension

I have helped develop the Layered Dark Sectors with a Structuring Field (LDS-SF) framework, in which the internal architecture of a multi-component dark sector generates scale-dependent growth of structure. The characteristic scale is set by the dominant eigenvalue of the dark-sector perturbation matrix, allowing the framework to suppress the matter power spectrum at late times and small scales while remaining indistinguishable from $ \Lambda $CDM at recombination. Implemented in a $ \mathbb{Z}_4 $-symmetric Inert Doublet-Singlet Model, this framework can bring the predicted $ S_8 $ amplitude into agreement with weak-lensing data from KiDS-1000 and DES.

3. BSM Dark Matter Phenomenology

I perform comparative computational analyses of dark matter candidates across extensions of the Standard Model: the Inert Doublet Model (IDM), the singlet-enhanced IDSM, the Three-Higgs Doublet Model (3HDM), the MSSM, and the NMSSM. My work maps the surviving parameter space after theoretical, precision-electroweak, collider, and cosmological constraints, and I have developed relic-density topology as a discriminatory tool for separating SUSY from non-SUSY dark matter candidates.

4. Cosmological Perturbation Theory

I work extensively with linear cosmological perturbation theory, including the derivation of the coupled CDM continuity and Euler equations in both Newtonian and conformal gauges, and the implementation of dark-sector perturbation equations in the CLASS Boltzmann solver. I also author pedagogical lecture notes on these topics, published open-access on Zenodo.

Methods and Tools

  • Field theory: SARAH, SPheno, CalcRGE, FeynCalc
  • Dark matter observables: micrOMEGAs
  • Cosmology: CLASS, custom perturbation solvers
  • Computation: Mathematica, Python, C++

Current Directions

My current work extends the LDS-SF framework to a mediator-mass hierarchy for dark-sector self-interactions (contact, Rutherford, and Yukawa regimes), and investigates how momentum-exchange-only dark-sector interactions imprint on CMB lensing and large-scale structure. A longer-term goal is to build a complete multi-component dark-sector phenomenology that connects particle-physics models to cosmological observables in a controlled, data-driven way.
